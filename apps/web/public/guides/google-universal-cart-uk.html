Google Universal Cart in the UK: what can you use now?

Google says its new Universal Cart is currently available only in the United States. If you're in the UK and mainly want somewhere to keep the products you find across different shops, you can still do that today. First decide whether you need a cart for checkout or a list for things you might buy later.

What Universal Cart does

Google's cart collects products you discover through Google Search and Shopping, AI Mode and Gemini. It can take you through checkout with participating merchants or send you to the merchant's site. That's useful if you want to move from discovery to buying inside Google's shopping flow.

A list for “maybe later” is a smaller job: keep the products together, close the tabs, and look again when you're ready.

For a handful of items, start with bookmarks

Make one Chrome bookmarks folder for products you're considering. It works across shops and takes seconds to set up. You'll need to reopen each page to check its price, though, and page titles aren't always easy to recognise.

If you want a saved list with price alerts

I built Tracer for that job. On a product page, open the Chrome extension and save the item. Finds from different shops sit in one list. Tracer can notify you when it verifies that a saved price has dropped.

It's free, and the list stays in that Chrome profile. It doesn't sync to your phone or check out for you. Some shops make prices difficult to verify, so an alert isn't guaranteed for every item. Tracer reports when it can't confirm a price instead of guessing.

If you need checkout across Google services, wait for Google's rollout in your country. If you need a shared gift list or phone sync, choose a tool built for that. Tracer is for saving products as you browse in Chrome and keeping an eye on their prices.
