How to save products from different shops in one place

If you browse a few shops before buying something, “save for later” quickly turns into open tabs, screenshots and links you can't find. There are a few ways to keep a shortlist. The right one depends on whether you just want the links or also want to know when a price changes.

1. Make a bookmarks folder

Create one folder for things you're considering and bookmark each product page. It's quick and works without adding another app.

The catch: a bookmark only remembers the page. You'll need to open each one again to see the current price, and it can be hard to recognise an item from a page title alone.

2. Keep a short note or spreadsheet

Put the product name, shop, link and price in one place. Add a line about why you saved it, such as “compare sizes” or “wait until next month.” This is especially useful if you're making a deliberate purchase decision.

You'll have to enter and update the details yourself. For a handful of items, that may be all you need.

3. Save from the product page

I built Tracer for the moment you find something you like but aren't ready to buy. Open the Chrome extension on a product page and save it. Products from different shops go into one list, and Tracer can alert you when it verifies a price drop.

Tracer is free. It runs in Chrome, and the saved list stays in that browser profile. It doesn't sync to your phone. Price checks depend on what the shop's page makes available, so a price won't always be verifiable.

If your main goal is a shared gift registry or a list that syncs across devices, choose a tool built for that. If you mostly shop on your computer and want to close the tabs without losing the products, Tracer may fit.
